Learner Rights and Protection

These summaries introduce important protections in learner-friendly language. They do not replace the full laws, DepEd issuances, or official procedures.

🧒

Right to Protection

Every child must be protected from abuse, exploitation, violence, neglect, and other forms of harm.

RA 7610 • DepEd Order 40, s. 2012
🚫

Protection from Bullying

Schools are required to establish measures to prevent and address bullying, including cyberbullying, consistent with applicable rules.

RA 10627 • DepEd Order 55, s. 2013
🏫

Safe Learning Environment

Learners should be able to study in an environment that promotes safety, dignity, respect, inclusion, and positive relationships.

See current DepEd issuances, including ESMLE policy
⚖️

Fair and Respectful Treatment

Learners should be treated with dignity. Discipline should follow applicable rules and should not involve degrading or harmful treatment.

🗣️

Right to Be Heard

Learners should have appropriate opportunities to express their views and be listened to in matters that affect them.

🤲

Right to Seek Help

Learners should be able to approach trusted adults and appropriate school protection personnel when they need support.

♿

Inclusion and Participation

Schools should work toward accessible, inclusive learning environments where learners can participate and receive appropriate support.

🔐

Privacy and Confidentiality

Personal information must be handled carefully and shared only through authorized processes and for legitimate purposes.

RA 10173 – Data Privacy Act of 2012
Remember: A learner who reports a concern should be treated with respect. Avoid blaming, shaming, threatening, or pressuring a learner to repeat a difficult experience unnecessarily.

Staying Safe and Seeking Support

Simple guidance for learners, families, and school personnel. Follow the applicable school and child-protection procedures when responding to an actual concern.

If you feel unsafe

  1. Move to a safer place if you can do so without increasing the danger.
  2. Tell a trusted adult, such as a parent, guardian, teacher, or school protection personnel.
  3. Explain what support you need. You may ask a trusted adult to accompany you.
  4. If there is immediate danger, seek urgent help from appropriate emergency services or authorities.

If another learner tells you about harm

  • Listen calmly and take the concern seriously.
  • Thank the learner for telling you.
  • Do not blame, interrogate, or promise absolute secrecy.
  • Connect the learner promptly with the designated school personnel and follow official procedures.
  • Share information only with people who need it to protect the learner and respond appropriately.

Keep online spaces safer

  • Think before sharing personal information, photos, or location details.
  • Do not forward humiliating, threatening, or harmful content.
  • Use available platform safety tools when appropriate.
  • Tell a trusted adult about threats, harassment, or cyberbullying.
  • Do not retaliate or put yourself at risk to collect evidence.
For school personnel: This page is general awareness material, not an investigation guide. Do not conduct an informal investigation or confront an alleged offender based solely on this website. Follow current DepEd issuances, designated reporting lines, referral protocols, and applicable safeguarding procedures.

Where to Get Help

If you feel unsafe or are worried about another learner, contact a trusted adult or the appropriate school personnel.

School Head

Provides school leadership and ensures that concerns are addressed through appropriate school procedures.

Guidance Designate / Counselor

May provide guidance support, help assess support needs within their role, and connect learners to appropriate services.

School LRP / Child Protection Personnel

Receives and refers concerns through authorized school procedures and coordinates appropriate action within assigned responsibilities.

Trusted Adult

A parent, guardian, teacher, or other responsible adult who can listen and help a learner reach appropriate support.

When sharing a concern: Provide information through the authorized school channel. Avoid posting details on social media or sending sensitive information through unapproved websites or messaging groups.
If there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services or appropriate child-protection authorities. Do not rely on this website for emergency response.
